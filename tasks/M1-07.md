## What the Massive plan allows (measured 2026-11-10)
- **History:** 1-minute aggregates back to **2024-11-11** only, a rolling 2 years. 2024-11-08 and earlier are refused: "Your plan doesn't include this data timeframe".
- **Rate:** about **5 requests per minute**. A 2nd quick call got HTTP 429.
- **WebSocket:** **not included**. Both `socket.massive.com` (real-time) and `delayed.massive.com` (15-minute delay) answered "Your plan doesn't include websocket access".
- **Venue:** the **consolidated US tape**. On 2026-11-05 regular hours, Massive's NVDA volume was 257.3M shares against 24.4M on TradingView's BATS export (10.6×); both had 390 minutes; closes were equal in 40% of minutes. This answers OI-27's question: the TradingView stock exports must come from a consolidated feed.
- **Bars:** `{t (ms, minute open), o, h, l, c, v, vw, n}`, with pre-market and after-hours included (the first bar is 04:00 New York). **Volume is fractional on every minute** (for example `149703.790541`): fractional-share trades on the tape.

## Decisions (Sham, 2026-11-10)
- **Volume:** raw keeps 6 decimals (`rawVolumeScale`). **Prices:** raw keeps up to 6 decimals too (`rawPriceScale`). First 4 was planned from one day's sample; then 2026-11-09 showed 6-decimal prints.
- **As traded:** `adjusted=false`; splits are applied when cooking (M1-08).
- **Live stream:** split out as **M1-07b**, blocked on **OI-28** (a Massive plan with WebSocket).
- **Settle:** a stock day is stored 2 days after it ends (crypto: 1 hour).
- **Re-fetch:** the 1,064 local raw files stored at the first (4-decimal) price scale were deleted and re-fetched.

## Plan (decisions marked ⚑ are Sham's)
**1. Backfill, now: `adapters/massive/massive-aggregates.ts`** implementing `HistoryProviderPort`, named `massive`.
- **Pages of many days in one request:** `GET /v2/aggs/ticker/{T}/range/1/minute/{fromMs}/{toMs}?adjusted=false&sort=asc&limit=50000`.
  - About 40 UTC days fit in one request, so two years for both symbols is roughly 30 requests, about 6 minutes at 5 per minute.
  - The response is split into UTC days, and `next_url` paging is followed if a page is full.
- **Pacing:** 13 s between requests, plus 429/5xx retries honouring `Retry-After`. The key goes in an `Authorization` header and never appears in error messages.
- **What gets stored:** every minute as received, pre/post included (the session filter belongs to cooking).
  - Raw is UTC days. In winter, after-hours to 20:00 New York spills into the next UTC day; that's fine.
  - Empty days (weekends, holidays) are not stored, as for crypto.
- **Prices:** scaled at 2 decimals, refusing more than the scale can hold.
- `Backfill` changes: it gets the pages through the port with a small `fetchDays(instrument, dates[])` extension, so one request serves 40 days. Crypto keeps calling `fetchDay`.
- **CLI:** `pnpm backfill --symbol NVDA --symbol TSLA` (stocks now allowed), default `--from 2024-11-11`.

**2. ⚑ Fractional volume.** Our stock instruments use `volumeScale: 0` (whole shares), so raw would refuse every Massive minute. Options:
- **(a, recommended)** Raw keeps Massive's 6 decimals, via a new optional `rawVolumeScale` on `Instrument` (shared-kernel, a contract change). Stocks get 6; crypto is unchanged. How cooked volume becomes whole shares is decided in M1-08, against a consolidated export. A day of NVDA at 6 decimals is about 1e15, inside the exact range. A *month* would not be (about 1e16), which is why cooked stays at 0.
- **(b)** Round to whole shares when storing. Simpler, but raw would no longer be exactly what Massive sent.

**3. ⚑ Adjusted or not.** Recommended: store **unadjusted** (`adjusted=false`). Raw then never changes when a split happens later. Split adjustment is applied when cooking (M1-08), from Massive's splits reference. There is no split for NVDA or TSLA inside 2024-11-11 to now, so nothing differs yet.

**4. ⚑ Live stream:** impossible on this plan (no WebSocket). Options:
- **(a, recommended)** Split it out: M1-07 is the backfill now, and a new task **M1-07b** (live stream) waits on a new owner input for a Massive plan with WebSocket. Starter has a 15-minute delay; real-time needs a higher tier. The choice links to OI-24, "data delay promised to customers".
- **(b)** Build the live client now against Massive's documented message format, tested only by replaying synthetic messages. That meets the "replay test" wording, but nothing proves it against the real feed until the plan changes.

**5. Tests:**
- parser (scaling, fractional volume per decision 2, out-of-range bars, duplicates);
- splitting a page into UTC days, including the after-hours spill and a DST change;
- paging via `next_url`;
- 429/`Retry-After`;
- the key absent from errors;
- `Backfill` across multi-day pages: a crash mid-page, then a re-run writing nothing new.

**6. Done-check run:** backfill NVDA and TSLA from 2024-11-11 into local MinIO, then re-run: 0 stored. Then a calendar cross-check: every trading day has bars, and no regular-hours bars fall on a holiday.

**7. Docs:** OI-27 gets the venue finding (consolidated tape; buy a consolidated TradingView feed, not BATS or Nasdaq-only). The `bar-rules.md` venue note is updated.

**Also for Sham (not this task):** 2 years of history means stock D/W/M "from listing" (NVDA 1999) cannot come from Massive on this plan, nor can the provider-daily-bars fallback in `bar-rules.md`. That matters for M1-08.

**Measured about Massive (the current plan)**

- **Venue: the consolidated tape**, 10.6× the BATS volume on 2026-11-05. This answers OI-27's question.

**Surprises**
1. Fractional volume and sub-penny prices on most minutes. Raw keeps them (new raw scales); cooking must round (M1-08).
2. A 4-decimal price scale, chosen from one day's sample, broke on yesterday's 6-decimal prints. Raw files carry no scale, so the 1,064 files stored at 4 decimals were deleted (with Sham's approval) and re-fetched at 6. **Lesson:** a raw scale must never change after data is written. If one ever must, raw needs a new provider prefix or scale metadata in the files.
3. Only the newest day had 6-decimal prints, so Massive's latest day looks unsettled; hence the 2-day settle for stocks.
