### The one real design decision: where do the 1-minute bars come from?
`docs/architecture.md` says cooked bars are "built **only from raw** 1-minute bars", and CLAUDE.md rule 3 says cooked must always be rebuildable from raw. But the raw store belongs to `contexts/hydration` and cooking belongs to `contexts/market-data`. Three ways:

- **(a) `market-data` imports `@aia/hydration`** and reads the raw store directly. Fewest moving parts, but makes the cooking context depend on the ingest context, and `contexts/market-data/AGENTS.md` says raw is explicitly *not* its own.
- **(b) Roll up from cooked 1m** instead, so `market-data` only ever reads its own store. Clean, but cooked 1m is then one step removed from raw, and M1-10 has to re-cook from raw anyway to mean anything.
- **(c) `market-data` declares a port it needs — `OneMinuteBarSourcePort` — and the app wires `hydration`'s raw store into it.** **My recommendation.** The context says what it needs without naming who provides it, CLAUDE.md rule 1 says `apps/` exist to wire things together, and M1-10 can wire the same port to raw to prove a rebuild. It also means these rollups can be tested against an in-memory source with no S3 at all, so the unit tests stay fast and hermetic.

This is a contract between contexts, which CLAUDE.md says to stop and ask about. **(c) unless you say otherwise.**

### Shape of the work
```
market-data/src/domain/roll-up.ts          pure: group 1m bars into one timeframe
market-data/src/domain/affected.ts         pure: which buckets a set of 1m bars touches
market-data/src/application/one-minute-bar-source-port.ts
market-data/src/application/cook-intraday.ts   the use case: read 1m, roll, write
```

Aggregation, from `docs/bar-rules.md`: open is the first 1-minute open, close the last 1-minute close, high the maximum of the highs, low the minimum of the lows, volume the sum in scaled whole units. Prices are **copied, never computed**, which is why parity on OHLC can be exact.

Bucketing is entirely `alignToTimeframe` from M0-04 — this task deliberately adds no new alignment logic, so stocks, DST, early closes and the 4h stub are already handled and already tested.

### Edge cases the tests must cover
- **A missing minute makes no bar and no hole**: a 5m bucket with only 3 of its 5 minutes present is still one bar, built from those 3 (`docs/bar-rules.md`: a minute with no trades has no bar, and we never invent flat bars).
- **A bucket with no minutes at all produces no bar**, rather than an empty one.
- **Incremental equals full**: cook a day minute by minute, then re-cook it in one pass, and the results must be identical.
- **One new minute touches one bucket per timeframe** — asserted by counting writes, since "only the affected bars" is the actual requirement and is otherwise easy to get silently wrong.
- **A late-arriving minute inside an already-cooked bucket** updates that bucket and leaves its neighbours untouched.
- **Boundaries**: the first and last minute of a bucket land in the right one; a minute at exactly 00:00 UTC opens a new 4h bar rather than closing the previous one.
- **Volume stays exact**: summing a full day of minutes at scale 8 matches the integer sum, which is the guarantee `shared-kernel`'s `sumVolume` exists to give.
- Although the row scopes the done-check to BTC/ETH, the same code path is exercised **once for a stock** too, to prove nothing in the rollup assumes 24/7 — the session-aware work proper is M1-08's.
