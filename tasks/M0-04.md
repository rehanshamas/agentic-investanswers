## Plan

### What exists now
`shared-kernel/src/index.ts` is a stub (`export {}`) with a comment naming this task. The package is tagged `type:kernel`, and `eslint.config.mjs` gives that tag `onlyDependOnLibsWithTags: []` — it may import nothing from the repo. `dependencies` is empty and stays empty.

### Design decisions
**1. `BarTime` is epoch seconds UTC, as a branded number.** `docs/bar-rules.md` stamps every bar with its open time in UTC and the exports carry UNIX epoch seconds, so seconds is the unit that makes parity an exact integer comparison with no rounding. Bar opens are always whole minutes, so sub-second precision would only add noise. Branding (`type BarTime = number & { readonly __brand: 'BarTime' }`) stops a raw `Date.now()` millisecond value being passed where a bar time belongs — a mistake that would otherwise typecheck and produce bars 1000x out.

**2. DST comes from `Intl`, not a dependency and not hand-rolled rules.** Stock sessions are defined in America/New_York and stored in UTC (bar-rules line 22), so something must know when New York is UTC-5 and when it is UTC-4. Three ways:
- `Intl.DateTimeFormat` with `timeZone: 'America/New_York'` — built in, backed by Node's ICU tzdata, correct for historic rules too. **Chosen.**
- Hard-code "second Sunday in March, first Sunday in November" — correct only from 2007 onward. Stock exports are "as much history as possible", so this would silently misalign older bars.
- Add `luxon` or `date-fns-tz` — needs your approval per CLAUDE.md, and buys nothing over `Intl` here.

This leans on Node shipping full ICU, which `docs/architecture.md` already pins to Node 22. A test asserts the offset on known dates so a stripped-ICU runtime fails loudly rather than silently.

**3. No `Date.now()` anywhere in this package.** Every helper takes the instant as an argument. Note the eslint rule that bans `Date.now` is scoped to `**/src/domain/**`, so it does **not** cover `shared-kernel/src`. I'll keep the rule by hand rather than widen `eslint.config.mjs` (out of scope for this task) — worth a follow-up to extend that glob.

### Alignment rules to implement (from `docs/bar-rules.md`)
| Timeframe | Crypto | Stocks |
|---|---|---|
| 1m, 5m, 15m, 1h | anchored to 00:00 UTC | anchored to session open; last bar of the day is short |
| 4h | 00:00/04:00/.../20:00 UTC | 09:30–13:30 and 13:30–16:00 NY; never spans two sessions |
| 1D | 00:00 to 24:00 UTC | the session's date |
| 1W | Monday 00:00 UTC | first session of the week (Tuesday after a Monday holiday) |
| 1M | calendar month, UTC | first session of the month, NY time |

### Tests (the done-check)
- Every intraday timeframe for crypto, including a bar straddling midnight UTC.
- Stock intraday anchored to the session open: 1h gives 09:30…15:30 with a 30-minute last bar; 4h gives exactly two bars, 09:30 and 13:30.
- **DST, both directions:** the session is 14:30–21:00 UTC in winter and 13:30–20:00 UTC in summer; a 1h bar on the spring-forward and autumn-back Sundays' following Mondays aligns correctly; a pre-2007 date proves the rules aren't hard-coded.
- A minute outside regular hours belongs to no bar (bar-rules: pre- and post-market minutes are not part of any bar).
- Holidays and early closes: a 13:00 early close ends the session; a holiday has no session; 1W rolls to Tuesday after a Monday holiday.
- `BarTime` rejects a millisecond-magnitude value.

### Approved 2026-11-02 (Sham)
All three decisions agreed, and precision settled now rather than later. So `Instrument` carries `priceScale` and `volumeScale`, and `Bar` holds prices and volume as scaled whole numbers (`scaled.ts`) — which is what makes the "no tolerance" rule in `docs/bar-rules.md` achievable rather than aspirational.

### Questions as asked, for the record
1. **`BarTime` in seconds, as above?** It's the contract every context inherits, so it's expensive to change later. I recommend seconds.
2. **Where does the NYSE holiday and early-close data live?** `docs/bar-rules.md` says "the calendar lives in config, one list per year", but the tests need real dates now. I propose: `MarketCalendar` is a *type* here and the helpers take a calendar as an argument, with a dated NYSE list for 2020–2026 shipped alongside as data. That keeps the kernel pure and gives M1-08 something real. The alternative is a fixture-only calendar in tests and the real data in M1-08.
3. **How much goes on `Instrument` now?** I'd keep it minimal — symbol, kind (crypto/stock), venue, session time zone — and *not* add price/volume precision yet, even though exact parity will need it (M1-02/M1-03). Say if you'd rather define precision now so the contract settles once.

**Surprises worth knowing.**
1. **`wei` was wrong in `docs/bar-rules.md`** — my own earlier wording. A month of ETH volume in wei is ~1e25, far past the 9e15 a float64 holds exactly, so it would have destroyed the exactness the rule exists to protect. Corrected to the export's precision (8 dp crypto, whole shares stocks), and `sumVolume` now fails loudly if a sum ever leaves the exact range.
2. **The eslint `Date.now` ban only covers `**/src/domain/**`**, so it does not protect `shared-kernel/src`. Kept by hand here. Widening that glob is a small follow-up, deliberately not done inside this task.
3. **RESOLVED 2026-11-08 (wrong guess, fixed in a follow-up PR).** Stock 1D/1W/1M bars are stamped with the **session open, 09:30 New York**, not midnight UTC — settled by `nvda_1d.csv`, whose 6,992 bars show one New York time of day and two UTC ones. The original note read: `docs/bar-rules.md` says these are "stamped with that session's date" while "Timestamps" says every bar carries its open time in UTC; for a daily stock bar those disagree. Midnight UTC of the session date is implemented, isolated in one function (`stampForSessionDate` in `align.ts`) so OI-04's exports settle it with a one-line change. The alternatives are midnight New York (05:00/04:00 UTC) or the 09:30 open (14:30/13:30 UTC).
4. **RESOLVED 2026-11-08.** The NYSE holiday data was validated against the real `nvda_1d.csv` export for 2021–2025: 1,255 trading days, **zero** days we claim as sessions that the market did not have, and zero the other way. That covers the unscheduled closures in range, including the 9 January 2025 Carter funeral. Still unverified outside 2020–2026, which is M1-12. The original note read: A wrong date is a real bar error. Parity will catch it loudly — a day we think is a session with no bars in the export — but it is worth 10 minutes against nyse.com before M1-08 leans on it.
